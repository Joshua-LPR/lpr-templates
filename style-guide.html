Voice & Copy Rules
1
Say the news, not the letter
The first sentence is the fact itself. Where “this letter serves as the required notice” carries legal value, it belongs in the legal footer — not the opener.
Replaces “This letter is to inform you that…”· “Please be advised that…”· “This letter serves as…”
2
Name the actor
Active voice, with a subject that acts. Passive reads as evasive exactly when trust matters most.
Replaces “there will be a monthly rent increase” → “your monthly rent will change to $X”· “deductions have been made” → “we deducted…”
3
Dates, not adverbs
Every deadline is a specific calendar date. Vague deadlines get filed, not acted on.
Replaces “as soon as possible”· “once available” → a specific date, e.g. “by August 1, 2026”
4
Plain words
Everyday language over formal or ledger jargon. Keep “premises” for the legal footer only.
Replaces “the premises” → “your home / the unit”· “outstanding ledger balance” → “the unpaid balance on your account”· “and/or” → “or”· “authorized personnel” → “our staff or a contractor”
5
One contact, one line
Every notice ends with the same closer — office contact, not personal. Process, not negotiation.
“Questions? Call the office at 443.402.5641 or email office@lasalleparkrealty.com.”
Replaces a personal cell number, or “call me directly” → the standard office line
6
Two closers, used consistently
“Sincerely,” for adverse or formal notices — rent increase, non-renewal, deposit withheld. “Thank you,” for service and good-news notices — entry notices, refunds.
Replaces ad-hoc or mismatched signoffs → one of the two standard closers
7
Five questions, every notice
What happened · when · why · what do I do · who do I ask. If any answer is missing, the letter isn't done. Target roughly an 8th-grade reading level, one idea per paragraph.
Replaces a notice that leaves one of the five unanswered → all five, answered in plain language
Notice Anatomy
1
Header — who it's from (logo, office contact)
2
Badge + Title — what kind of notice, how urgent
Skim
3
Date + recipient address block — proof of service
4
Opening line — the point, in one sentence
5
Key-facts block — amounts, dates, addresses (the info-grid)
Skim
6
Detail paragraphs — the why and the context
7
Action — what to do + a specific deadline
Skim
8
Signoff — name, title, LLC
9
Legal footer — statute / lease citation
A notice is skimmed for 5 seconds before it's read for 5 minutes. If a tenant reads only rows 2, 5, and 7 — the gold skim path — they should still know everything they need. The prose exists for completeness and tone, not for carrying the facts.
Callout Semantics

Blue Callout — Action Required

Pay the $1,240.00 balance by August 1, 2026 to avoid further action.
Something the tenant must doAn action with a deadline.
bg rgba(40,56,145,0.05) · border-left 3px var(--lpr-blue)

Gold Callout — Key Figures & Dates

New monthly rent: $1,595.00 · effective October 1, 2026.
Information to anchor onAmounts, dates, addresses.
bg rgba(214,163,90,0.10) · border-left 3px #d6a35a
Consistency is the whole point. Semantic color only works if it never drifts. A tenant who receives several LPR letters learns the code — blue means do something, gold means here are the numbers. Tinting a paragraph gold for emphasis, or boxing routine text in blue, breaks the signal for every notice that follows.
